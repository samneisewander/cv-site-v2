## About

Hello! I'm Sam, a senior at the
[University of Notre Dame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Notre_Dame)
majoring in Computer Science and minoring in Music. I'm from
[Dixon, IL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dixon,_Illinois#), and on campus I'm a
resident of
[Sorin College](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorin_Hall_(University_of_Notre_Dame)>).

I'm interested in low-level computing. I'm most proficient in C and Go, and I
enjoy studying operating systems, compilers, networking, distributed systems,
and security. I also enjoy self-hosting! I have a NAS box running a few
services, like Immich and Jellyfin. I like the process of tinkering and doing
sysadmin work. I'm also interested in open source software, and am the president
of the [Notre Dame Linux User Group](https://ndlug.org).

Besides computing, I also love music. I'm studying for a music minor, taking
theory, history, and piano lessons. I've worked as a producer on a couple of
projects, and I like to write, record, and produce my own music as well. I have
performed for a few campus events during my time at Notre Dame, and for a few
years I was a member and music director for a small a cappella club.

My hobbies include reading, hiking, lifting, running, cooking/baking, and video
games.

## Experience

### Volexity

Last summer, I worked as a software engineering intern at
[Volexity](https://www.volexity.com/). I loved my time here! The work I did was
fascinating and my team was excellent. My projects included developing product
features in Go, and working on build tooling for developer environments and
CI/CD using Docker and Makefiles.

### University of Notre Dame CSE Department

I have worked (or am working) as a TA for a few classes at Notre Dame,
including:

- Data Structures for Professor Jay Brockman (Fall 2025)
- Systems Programming for Professor Peter Bui (Spring 2026)
- Operating Systems for Professor Peter Bui (Fall 2026)

Being a TA has been a particularly fulfilling experience. It has sharpened my
technical knowledge, improved my communication and mentorship abilities, and has
given me a chance to practice my code reviewing skills and to give useful
feedback to young programmers. A part of the TA role that I most enjoy is
connecting with underclassmen and inviting them to participate in the department
culture, explore the fields of computer science they are passionate about, and
help them discern decisions regarding their career or curriculum choices. I find
that being a TA gives me a platform I can use to have a large positive effect on
these young students.

### iD Tech Camps

I spent a few weeks during the summer of 2025 working as a course instructor for
iD Tech Camps, a company that hosts week-long, STEM-related summer camps for
kids in middle school and high school. I taught a class on programming with
Python, and another class on 3D modelling and 3D printing.

This job really challenged my ability to think on my feet. I had to make
last-minute curriculum adjustments, respond to emergencies, and resolve
conflicts all while maintaining a fun, safe, and inviting atmosphere at the
camp. My teammates were amazing; without their guidance and support, I would not
have been nearly as successful as I was.
